# Burford v. Sun Oil Co

## Citation and Court
Burford v. Sun Oil Co., 319 U.S. 315 (1943). United States Supreme Court.

## Facts
Sun Oil Company challenged a Texas Railroad Commission order granting a drilling permit to a competitor in the East Texas oil field. Sun Oil brought suit in federal court, invoking diversity jurisdiction. Texas had created a specialized system of regulation and review of oil and gas matters, with all appeals channeled through a single state court for consistency. Intervention by federal courts risked disrupting this coordinated state regulatory regime.

## Issue
Whether a federal court sitting in diversity should abstain from hearing a challenge to a state agency order where the case involves difficult questions of state law embedded in a complex state regulatory system.

## Holding
The Supreme Court held that the district court abused its discretion by retaining jurisdiction and directed it to dismiss. Federal intervention into Texas's oil regulation scheme would cause needless friction with the state's specialized administrative and judicial system.

## Rule / Doctrine
Burford abstention: a federal court may (and sometimes must) abstain and dismiss when a case presents (1) difficult questions of state law bearing on state policy problems of substantial public import, or (2) where federal court adjudication would disrupt state efforts to establish a coherent policy on a matter of substantial concern. The doctrine is discretionary and applies primarily where a state has created specialized procedures for uniform resolution of the contested legal questions.

## Significance
Burford abstention is a tool for preserving coherent state regulatory authority when federal adjudication would fragment or undermine specialized state regulatory schemes. It is less common than Pullman or Younger abstention and is closely associated with cases involving complex state administrative systems such as energy regulation, insurance, or land use.
