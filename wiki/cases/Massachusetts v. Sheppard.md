# Massachusetts v. Sheppard

## Citation
468 U.S. 981 (1984)

## Facts
An officer used a pre-printed search warrant form designed for controlled substances to search for evidence of a murder; the magistrate acknowledged the form was wrong but said he would make it work; the warrant was later found to be technically defective.

## Issue
Whether the exclusionary rule requires suppression of evidence seized pursuant to a defective warrant when the officer reasonably relied on the magistrate's assurance that the warrant was valid.

## Holding
The Court held the evidence admissible because the officer acted in good faith, objectively reasonable reliance on the magistrate's determination that the warrant was valid.

## Rule
Good faith exception — the exclusionary rule does not apply when an officer acts in objectively reasonable reliance on a warrant issued by a magistrate, even if the warrant is later found defective; decided the same day as *United States v. Leon*.
