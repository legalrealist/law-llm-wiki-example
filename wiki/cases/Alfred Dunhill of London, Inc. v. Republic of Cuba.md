# Alfred Dunhill of London, Inc. v. Republic of Cuba

## Citation and Court
425 U.S. 682 (1976), Supreme Court of the United States

## Facts
After Cuba nationalized the cigar industry, Cuban state-controlled entities sold cigars to American importers including Alfred Dunhill. The importers had already paid for cigars to the pre-nationalization owners and sought repayment of those funds. Cuban officials refused to return the money and claimed the refusal was an act of state.

## Issue
Whether the act of state doctrine bars a claim based on Cuba's repudiation of a commercial obligation incurred by Cuban state enterprises.

## Holding
The act of state doctrine does not bar claims based on the commercial acts of a foreign sovereign; a plurality recognized a commercial activity exception to the doctrine.

## Rule / Doctrine
The act of state doctrine applies to sovereign, governmental acts of a foreign state taken within its own territory, but a plurality of the Court held that purely commercial conduct by a foreign state is not shielded from judicial scrutiny under the doctrine.

## Significance
The case is significant for recognizing (in plurality) a commercial exception to the act of state doctrine, paralleling the commercial activity exception in the FSIA. It signals that courts need not abstain from evaluating a foreign sovereign's commercial transactions the way they must abstain from reviewing governmental acts.
