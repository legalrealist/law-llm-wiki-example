# Canadian Industrial Alcohol Co. v. Dunbar Molasses Co.

## Citation
258 N.Y. 194 (N.Y. Court of Appeals, 1932)

## Facts
Dunbar Molasses contracted to supply a quantity of molasses to Canadian Industrial Alcohol but failed to deliver when its expected source — a particular refinery — did not produce enough. Dunbar claimed excuse based on its inability to procure the molasses. The contract did not expressly condition Dunbar's performance on supply from any specific source.

## Issue
Whether a seller is excused from its contractual obligation to deliver goods when it is unable to procure sufficient supply from a third-party source not expressly named in the contract.

## Holding
The court held that Dunbar was not excused; the seller bore the risk of its inability to procure the molasses and was liable for breach.

## Rule
A seller cannot be excused for failure to deliver based on inability to procure goods from a specific source unless the contract expressly conditions performance on that source; vague assumption that supply would be available is insufficient.
