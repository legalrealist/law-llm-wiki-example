# AT&T Mobility LLC v. Concepcion

## Citation and Court
563 U.S. 333 (2011), Supreme Court of the United States

## Facts
The Concepcions entered a wireless contract with AT&T Mobility containing an arbitration clause and a class arbitration waiver. When they alleged AT&T had committed fraud, they sought to pursue a class action. California courts held the class arbitration waiver unconscionable under California law. AT&T argued the FAA preempted California's rule.

## Issue
Whether the Federal Arbitration Act preempts California's rule that class arbitration waivers in consumer contracts are unconscionable and therefore unenforceable.

## Holding
The FAA preempts California's Discover Bank rule barring class arbitration waivers; state rules that disproportionately disfavor arbitration — even when framed as general contract doctrines — are preempted by the FAA.

## Rule / Doctrine
The FAA reflects a strong national policy favoring arbitration agreements and preempts state law rules that "stand as an obstacle" to the enforcement of arbitration agreements according to their terms. A state unconscionability rule that effectively bars class waivers in arbitration agreements is preempted, even if framed neutrally, because it places arbitration on unequal footing with other contracts.

## Significance
Concepcion dramatically expanded the enforceability of class arbitration waivers in consumer and employment contracts, triggering a wave of litigation over FAA preemption of state law. It is now a foundational case for both arbitration law and the conflict between federal preemption and state consumer protection policy.
