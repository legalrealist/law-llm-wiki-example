# Conley v. Gibson

**Citation**: 355 U.S. 41 (1957)

## Facts

African-American railroad workers sued their union under the Railway Labor Act, alleging racial discrimination. The district court dismissed, finding the complaint insufficient. The Supreme Court reversed.

## Issue

What standard governs the sufficiency of a complaint under the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure?

## Holding

A complaint should not be dismissed for failure to state a claim unless it appears beyond doubt that the plaintiff can prove no set of facts in support of the claim that would entitle relief. Liberal "notice pleading" requires only a short and plain statement of the claim.

## Rule

**No-set-of-facts standard (notice pleading)**: A complaint survives a motion to dismiss unless there is "no set of facts" that could entitle the plaintiff to relief. The complaint need not include detailed factual allegations; it need only give fair notice of the claim and the grounds on which it rests.

**Overruled by**: *Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly* (2007) and *Ashcroft v. Iqbal* (2009) replaced this with the plausibility pleading standard — a complaint must plead factual content that allows the court to draw the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable.

## Significance

- Defined the liberal notice-pleading standard that governed federal courts for 50 years
- Expressly overruled on the pleading standard by *Twombly* and *Iqbal*, though the "short and plain statement" requirement of FRCP 8(a)(2) remains
- Historical baseline for understanding how the pleading standard changed dramatically in 2007–2009
