# Hurtado v. Superior Court

**Citation:** 11 Cal.3d 574 (Cal. 1974)

## Facts
Mexican citizens were killed in California in an automobile accident caused by a California defendant. Under Mexican law, wrongful death damages were limited to a small fixed amount; California imposed no such cap. The defendants argued that Mexican law should limit the recovery of the Mexican plaintiffs.

## Issue
Which state's law governs the measure of wrongful death damages when the plaintiff-decedents are Mexican domiciliaries and the accident and the defendant are in California?

## Holding
California law applies. The California Supreme Court held that California had the stronger interest in applying its own unlimited damages rule because Mexico had no interest in limiting recovery for its citizens against a California tortfeasor — Mexico's cap was designed to protect Mexican defendants, not to limit recovery by Mexican victims.

## Rule
Under California's comparative impairment approach (a variant of interest analysis), courts ask which state's interest would be more impaired if its law were not applied. When a state's law is designed to protect a particular party who is not present in the litigation, that state's interest is not impaired by failing to apply its law.

## Significance
Hurtado illustrates California's comparative impairment methodology and the importance of identifying the purpose behind each state's law before assessing its interest. The case is paired with Babcock and Schultz to show differing state approaches to interest analysis.
