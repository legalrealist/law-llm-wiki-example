# Railroad Commission of Texas v. Pullman Co

## Citation and Court
Railroad Commission of Texas v. Pullman Co., 312 U.S. 496 (1941). United States Supreme Court.

## Facts
The Texas Railroad Commission issued an order requiring that a Pullman Company sleeping car porter — a job held by Black workers — be supervised by a conductor, a job held by white workers. The Pullman Company and sleeping-car porters challenged the order in federal court on both state-law and federal constitutional (equal protection) grounds. The Texas statute authorizing the order was ambiguous, and if construed narrowly might not authorize the order at all, which would moot the constitutional question.

## Issue
Whether a federal court should proceed to decide a federal constitutional question when the case also involves an unsettled question of state law whose resolution might eliminate the need for constitutional adjudication.

## Holding
The Supreme Court unanimously held that the district court should stay the federal proceedings pending a state court determination of the state-law question. Resolution of the ambiguous state law question might make the federal constitutional question unnecessary.

## Rule / Doctrine
Pullman abstention: a federal court should stay — not dismiss — proceedings and defer to state courts for clarification of an unsettled state law question when (1) the state law is genuinely ambiguous or unclear, (2) a state court's clarification could resolve or narrow the federal constitutional question, and (3) constitutional adjudication can thereby be avoided. The federal case is stayed, not dismissed, preserving the plaintiff's right to return to federal court.

## Significance
Pullman abstention embodies the constitutional avoidance principle at the level of jurisdiction: federal courts should not decide constitutional questions unnecessarily. It also preserves comity with state courts. The doctrine differs from Burford abstention (which addresses regulatory coherence) and Younger abstention (which addresses pending state proceedings).
