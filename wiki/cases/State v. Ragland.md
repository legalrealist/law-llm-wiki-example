# State v. Ragland

## Citation and Court
105 N.J. 189 (New Jersey Supreme Court 1986)

## Facts
Ragland was charged with receiving stolen property and possession of a weapon. He raised voluntary intoxication as a defense, arguing it negated the requisite mental state. The trial court gave a jury instruction that arguably shifted the burden on the intoxication defense and may have misled jurors about how to weigh the evidence of intoxication.

## Issue
Whether a jury instruction on voluntary intoxication that improperly limited the jury's consideration of that evidence constituted reversible error.

## Holding
The court found the jury instruction on intoxication was erroneous but analyzed whether the error was harmless; the case is primarily cited for its treatment of the relationship between voluntary intoxication, specific intent, and jury instructions.

## Rule / Doctrine
Voluntary intoxication is relevant to whether a defendant formed the specific intent required by certain crimes. A jury instruction that improperly restricts jurors' ability to consider intoxication evidence in deciding whether the prosecution proved the requisite mens rea beyond a reasonable doubt may violate due process. Courts distinguish between general-intent crimes (where voluntary intoxication is typically not a defense) and specific-intent crimes (where it may negate the required mental state).

## Significance
A New Jersey case used in criminal law courses to examine the scope of the voluntary intoxication defense and the constitutional requirements for jury instructions on mental state defenses.
